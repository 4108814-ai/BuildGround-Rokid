Surface list rows

A card used to be a title and some lines. It can now be a list: rows with a second line under them, a weight that says how much they matter, and a selection the HUD draws itself.

The rule that has not moved: a plugin supplies structure, the platform supplies style. You say which row is selected and what each one is worth. You never say how big, how bright, or how far apart.

1

Plain lines

What every card did before, and still does. Pass lines and nothing else: left-aligned monospace, no selection, no hierarchy. Right for a block of text, wrong for a list of things.

Body rows: a conversation

A BODY row wraps over as many lines as it needs and puts its badge in a label column to the left. That is the shape of a conversation: who said it, then what they said.

Leave the label empty when it would repeat the title. In a one-to-one thread the title is already that person's name, so a label on every row restates it down the whole screen. In a group the title is the group and the labels are the only thing saying who is speaking — that is where they earn their column.

5

What the platform still owns

Everything about how this looks. A plugin that finds itself counting pixels or columns is working around the tier rather than with it.

YoursOurs
Which row is selectedHow selection is drawn
What each row is worth (tone)Size, weight, colour
The text and its sub lineWrapping, spacing, alignment
The order of the rowsHow many fit, and what happens past that

One number is worth knowing while you write copy: a row reads comfortably up to about twenty-six monospace columns on the optics. Past that it wraps, and a wrapped row breaks the one-line-per-thing rhythm that makes a list scannable. Put the short thing in text and the long thing in sub.
